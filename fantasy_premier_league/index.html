FPL five-factor picks

An additive binary-factor model for weekly Fantasy Premier League picks — one star per factor, judged against position peers, in the family of march_madness/ and nfl_report/.

The five factors

QQualityModel expected points per 90 — rebuilt from the FPL scoring rules and the player's underlying per-90 numbers (xG, xA, xGC, defensive contributions, saves) — above the position median.
VValueExpected points per 90 per £million above the position median. Points per pound funds the rest of the squad.
FFormPoints over the last 5 gameweeks above the position median. Momentum.
JJusticeUnder-rewarded over the last 6 gameweeks: attackers whose xGI beats their actual returns, defenders/keepers who conceded more than their xGC. Luck mean-reverts; the unlucky are cheap.
CCrowdOwnership percentile below quality percentile within the position — the field underweights the player (bet against beta).

Eligibility gate: 45+ minutes per gameweek averaged over the last 4 gameweeks — no factor can rescue a player who doesn't play. Full sorted leaderboards ↓ · Worked examples of every factor ↓

How each factor is calculated — worked examples

Every number in this section is made up, chosen so the sums are easy to follow. The real model runs exactly this arithmetic over every eligible player, inside each position (GK / DEF / MID / FWD).

Q Quality — worked example

Take a made-up midfielder, Player A. Over the season his per-90 underlying numbers are: xG 0.40, xA 0.20, team xGC while he's on the pitch 1.20, and he hits the 12-action defensive-contribution threshold in 35% of his starts. Apply the FPL scoring rules to those rates:

ComponentCalculationPoints/90
Goals0.40 × 5 (MID goal)2.00
Assists0.20 × 30.60
Clean sheetse−1.20 ≈ 0.30 × 1 (MID CS)0.30
Defensive contribution0.35 × 20.70
Model xPts/903.60

Do that for every eligible midfielder, then compare each to the position median. Among our five made-up midfielders the median is 2.6 — the star goes to everyone strictly above it:

PlayerxPts/90Star?
Player A3.6★ yes
Player B2.6no
Player C4.1★ yes
Player D1.8no
Player E2.2no

Player B sits exactly on the median, so no star — "above the median" is strict. Note the engine never looks at actual points scored: goals in, luck out.

Divide each player's xPts/90 by his price. Player A: 3.6 ÷ £8.0m = 0.45 points per 90 per £million.

The median is 0.40, so Players A and B collect the star. The teaching point is Player C: the best player on the list (4.1 xPts/90) but at £14.0m he returns only 0.29 per £m — top of the Quality table, yet no Value star. Players D and E sit exactly on the median: no star.

J Justice — worked example

Player F has banked 3.7 expected goal involvements but cashed only 2 — he is due, so he gets the star. Player G has scored far above his numbers — that usually washes out, so no star. Defenders add a second ledger: Player H's team conceded 9 against an xGC of 7.0, so his side has been unlucky at the back too (goalkeepers use only that second ledger). Any margin above zero earns the star.

The minutes gate — worked example

Before any factor is scored, a player must average 45+ minutes per gameweek over the last 4 gameweeks (180 in total). Minutes of 90, 90, 30, 75 = 285 → rated. Minutes of 60, 0, 90, 20 = 170 → not rated at all, whatever the underlying numbers say. No factor can rescue a player who doesn't play.
