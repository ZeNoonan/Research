Read this first

Where the numbers come from. No single source has all three. fbref publishes shots and penalties attempted, one sheet per gameweek, each one cumulative to the end of that gameweek — so a single gameweek is the sheet minus its predecessor. The FPL API publishes expected goals, expected assists and minutes, already week by week. The two are joined player by player; the join is audited at the foot of the page.

Penalties. A penalty is worth about 0.79 xG in the models, and it tells you who takes penalties rather than who is getting shots away. Left in, one spot kick outranks four good chances. So each attempt fbref records costs its taker 0.75 xG in that gameweek, and the ranking uses what is left. The weeks it applied are listed below, and marked with a small red p in the tables.

How the ranking works. Within a gameweek, over the players who actually played, each of shots, adjusted xG and xA is ranked with 1 the best; ties share the mean rank, so nobody gains by sitting in a crowd of zeros. The three ranks are added and the sum re-ranked from 1 — that number is the cell on the Aggregate ranking tab. The other three tabs show the raw number with its own rank in that category in brackets beside it, which is the rank that belongs with a shots count — the aggregate is two thirds about the other two, and has its own tab. Hover any cell for all of it: the three category ranks, the raw numbers, and what they summed to.

A player who did not play is blank (—), not zero and not last: he was injured, rested, suspended or an unused substitute, and the data does not say which. But a column of blanks cannot be summed honestly, because missing a gameweek would then improve a player's total. So Total charges a missed gameweek that week's last place, and Avg is the mean over the gameweeks he actually played. They answer different questions — Total asks who has been most useful so far, Avg asks who is best when he plays — and GWs sits between them so you can always see which is which. Sorting is on any column; blanks always sink.

Totals, not rates. A substitute who plays ten minutes is ranked against a man who played ninety, on the same raw numbers. That is the honest way to answer "who did most this gameweek", but it is not "who is best per minute" — a cameo with one shot in it will outrank a quiet full ninety. Mins is on every row so you can see which you are looking at.

What the bottom of the table means. GW1 76 (25%); GW2 75 (24%) of the players who played took no shot, made no chance and were credited with no expected goals at all. They are genuinely tied, they all share the same rank, and no ordering among them means anything. The field each week: GW1 310; GW2 312.

Adding a gameweek. Drop a new GW<n> sheet into data/2026-27/fbref_shots.xlsx — cumulative, like the others — re-run scraper_fpl.py for the FPL side, then python build_shots.py. The sheet names are the gameweek numbers; the workbook's own Week column is a row rank and is ignored.

How the two sources were joined

fbref writes display names (Bruno Fernandes); FPL writes first_second lowercased (bruno_borges fernandes). Names are normalised and assigned one-to-one, best match first. A club agreeing helps but is not required — FPL reports a player's current club, so a deadline-day move disagrees with the club he actually played for. Whatever is left over is settled on minutes, which is what catches the nicknames no string match will: fbref's Beto is FPL's norberto bercique gomes betuncal, its Costinha is joão pedro loureiro da costa.

Minutes then audit every pair. Two sources that counted the same man's minutes independently are the check on whether the join is right, and anything they disagree on by more than 10 minutes is named here rather than quietly shipped. So is anyone FPL says was on the pitch that fbref does not list, because he would otherwise be ranked as having taken no shot when the truth is that nobody counted them.
