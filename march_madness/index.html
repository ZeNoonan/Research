The Variance-difference anomaly (and what was fixed)

The Slot calculations tab, columns AL:AQ (“Variance difference”), computes the variance gained by picking team i instead of the slot favorite. The paper’s formula is ΔVar = (p−p₀)(1+2S) − p²+p₀² − 2(pf − p₀f₀), where S is the slot’s expected wins for the average entry — the value already computed in column B of the same row.

Bug 1 (the one you spotted): AL3 reads (1+2*B41) — the S of the team 38 rows further down — instead of (1+2*B3). Filled down, the reference walks off the data: from row 29 onward it points at empty cells, so more than half the teams were computed with S = 0. Fixed to the same-row B3 reference.

Bug 2 (surfaced by fixing bug 1): the Value flag (BD:BI) tests ratio > 1, where ratio = cumulative ΔVar ÷ cumulative ΔEV. But the sheet stores ΔEV as a negative number for non-favorites, so the paper’s rule — “variance gain at least as large as the expected-value loss” — corresponds to ratio ≤ −1. As published, the flag fired only for picks whose variance loss exceeded the EV loss: the exact opposite. Fixed to IF(OR(ratio=0, ratio≤−1),1,0).

Effect: 100 of 384 Value flags change (98 gained, 2 lost). As published, the only championship-column Value pick was Duke. Fixed, the championship Value picks are Duke, Virginia Tech, Michigan State, Gonzaga, Texas Tech, Michigan, Virginia and Kentucky — and the 2019 tournament was won by Virginia over Texas Tech, with Michigan State also in the Final Four. Texas Tech becomes the only 5-star champion pick on the board. Use the “As published” toggle above to compare; changed cells are outlined in gold.

The five factors

Quality

ESPN Basketball Power Index above the median of the four teams sharing the seed. High-BPI halves of each seed won 1.35 games per tournament vs 0.58 for low-BPI halves (1986–2017).

Size

Recent tournament success (exponentially weighted) above seed median. Surprisingly, the “big”, familiar programs outperform: 24 of 32 champions were top-two in size for their seed.

Momentum

Wins in the last five regular-season games. Good for top-8 seeds (want 4–5); inverted for bottom-8 seeds (want ≤3), because weak finishers there earned their bid without an automatic conference win.

Value

Slot-level: the pick increases the variance of your score versus the pool average by at least as much as it costs in expected points — or it is the favorite. Every champion since 1985 has been a Value pick.

Bet against beta

Slot-level: a non-favorite within 0.5 win probability of the favorite that the field picks less often relative to its probability. Differentiates your bracket without Gaussian assumptions.

Data notes

  • Probabilities, pick frequencies, factor values, default picks, round points and pool size all come from the March 18, 2019 workbook (ESPN pool data for frequencies).
  • Play-in slots (NDS/NCC, FD/PV, Bel/Tem, AS/SJ) carry the average Quality/Size/Momentum of the two candidate teams, as in the workbook.
