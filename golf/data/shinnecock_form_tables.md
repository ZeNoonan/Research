## Reading the table for Shinnecock

A made-cut rate alone flatters grinders. Harman and Berger both sit at 100% across their verified events, but Harman has just two top-10s (his Hoylake win aside) and Berger none — they survive proxy setups without threatening them. For a Flynn links test where the winner has to *contend* in wind on firm ground, finish quality in the heaviest proxies (the three Opens, the Scottish, LACC and Pinehurst) matters more than simply playing the weekend.

Through that lens the form points clearly at a small group. **Scheffler** is the outlier: 11 weekends from 11, nine of them top-10s, and the win at Portrush — he has no soft spot anywhere on the board, including the links rotation. **Schauffele** is the other anchor — a perfect 12/12, five top-10s, the Troon Claret Jug, and a Pinehurst T7 that shows the same game travels to a firm USGA setup. **McIlroy** carries eight top-10s, the 2023 Scottish title and the LACC runner-up, with only the Troon missed cut against him. **Rahm** belongs in this tier on quality (T2 Hoylake, T7 Troon, T7 Oakmont) even though his post-LIV schedule leaves only five verified proxy events.

The most distinctive Shinnecock profile, though, is **MacIntyre**. He is a genuine wind-and-links specialist: runner-up at the 2023 Scottish, the 2024 Scottish title, T7 at Portrush, and — most tellingly for a US Open read — solo second at Oakmont in the year's hardest setup. His only gap on the board is the single unverified LACC cell. Behind that group, **Henley** (five top-10s, Troon 5th, three major top-10s), **Fleetwood** (deep links pedigree but still winless and two proxy missed cuts), **Lowry** (6th at Troon, strong on the Irish/Scottish links) and **Conners** (relentless ball-striker, T9 Pinehurst, T10 Portrush) are the next most credible.

Two honest caveats sit over all of it. First, the LIV-aligned players — Rahm, DeChambeau, Koepka, Cameron Smith, Niemann, Reed and Dustin Johnson — are `NIF` for most of the PGA Tour limited-field proxies, so their rates rest on five or six events and aren't directly comparable to the full-schedule players. Second, the three Pebble columns and Oakmont are the weakest architectural proxies (coastal-but-short, weather-shortened, no-cut, or simply parkland-penal rather than links), so weight them lightly; the Opens, the Scottish, LACC and Pinehurst carry the real signal. The ten residual `?` cells are all fringe field-status questions for low-event players and don't move any of the conclusions above.
