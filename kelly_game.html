What is the Kelly Criterion?

The Kelly Criterion is a mathematical formula that determines the optimal bet size to maximize long-term wealth growth while minimizing risk of ruin.

f* = (bp - q) / b
Where:
  • f* = optimal fraction of bankroll to bet
  • b = net odds (payout ratio)
  • p = probability of winning
  • q = probability of losing (1 - p)
Example (60% win rate, even money):
f* = (1.0 × 0.6 - 0.4) / 1.0 = 0.2 or 20%
→ You should bet 20% of your bankroll!

Why It Works

✓ Optimal Growth: Maximizes long-term wealth growth rate
⚠️ Betting Too Much: Increases risk of ruin, even with an edge
⚠️ Betting Too Little: Leaves profits on the table, slower growth

Practical Considerations

Full Kelly: Maximum growth, but high volatility
Half Kelly: 75% of growth, 50% of volatility
Quarter Kelly: More stable, much slower growth

Many professional gamblers use Half Kelly or Quarter Kelly to reduce psychological stress and account for estimation errors.
